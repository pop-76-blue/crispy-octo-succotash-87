1.3 Position-Time Graphs

Reading a d-t graph, and finding velocity from its slope.

A position-time (d-t) graph lets you do two things:

  1. Read it to find the instantaneous position at any time.
  2. Find the slope to get velocity.

If the graph is a straight line, the slope between any two points is the same — that's constant velocity, and average velocity = instantaneous velocity everywhere.

If the graph is curved, the slope changes over time — the object is accelerating. To find velocity you now have two choices:

  • Slope between two points on the curve (a secant line) → average velocity over that interval.
  • Slope of a tangent at one single point → instantaneous velocity at that moment.
