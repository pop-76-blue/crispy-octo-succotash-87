Quick Reference

Significant Figures

1–9 always count. Zeros between digits count. Leading zeros never count. Trailing zeros count only if there's a decimal point. Everything in scientific notation counts.

Scientific Notation

a × 10n, where 1 ≤ a < 10. Multiply: add exponents. Divide: subtract exponents. Power: multiply the exponent, raise the coefficient.

Distance & Displacement

Distance only adds up (scalar). Displacement is final position minus initial position (vector) — it can cancel out on a round trip.

Speed & Velocity

Speed = total distance ÷ time (scalar). Velocity = displacement ÷ time (vector). They're only equal if you never change direction.

Position-Time Graphs

Straight line = constant velocity. Slope between two points = average velocity. Curved graph: slope of a tangent at one point = instantaneous velocity.

Sig Figs
How many significant digits are in 0.070 40?
4 significant digits. The two leading zeros don't count (rule 3), but the zero between 7 and 4, and the trailing zero after the decimal point, both count (rules 2 and 4).
Sci. Notation
Convert 0.000 000 56 to scientific notation.
5.6 × 10-7
Sci. Notation
Simplify: (3.2 × 104) × (5 × 10-2)
(3.2 × 5) × 104+(-2) = 16 × 102 = 1.6 × 103
Displacement
A cyclist rides 12 km east, then turns around and rides 5 km west. Find the total distance travelled and the total displacement.
Distance = 12 + 5 = 17 km.
Displacement = 12 km [E] − 5 km [E] = 7 km [E] (since 5 km west is −5 km [E]).
Speed & Velocity
A runner completes one lap of a 400 m track in 52 s, finishing back at the start. Find the average speed and average velocity.
Average speed = 400 m ÷ 52 s ≈ 7.7 m/s.
Average velocity = 0 m/s — the displacement is zero because the runner ends up back where they started.
Position-Time Graphs
A position-time graph is a straight line from (0 s, 2 m) to (6 s, 26 m). Find the velocity.
v = Δd / Δt = (26 m − 2 m) / (6 s − 0 s) = 24 m / 6 s = 4 m/s
Position-Time Graphs
On a curved (non-uniform) d-t graph, what's the difference between finding average velocity and instantaneous velocity?
Average velocity uses a secant line between two points on the curve. Instantaneous velocity uses a tangent line at a single point — a straight line that touches the curve at just that one spot, going in the same direction as the curve there.
