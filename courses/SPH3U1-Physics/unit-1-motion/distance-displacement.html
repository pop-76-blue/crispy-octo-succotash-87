1.1 Distance & Displacement

Scalars, vectors, and the difference between "how far you walked" and "where you ended up."

Scalars vs. Vectors

Scalars are quantities with only a magnitude (a value) — e.g. temperature, mass, distance.

Vectors are quantities with both a magnitude and a direction — e.g. displacement, velocity, force.

In this course we write a direction in square brackets, like 13 m [right] or 35 km [N30°W].

Distance vs. Displacement

Distance is the total length of the path travelled — it only adds up, it never subtracts.

Position (d) is your distance and direction from a reference point.

Displacement (Δd) is the change in position: Δd = dfinal − dinitial. It only cares about start and end — not the path in between.

If you change position more than once, total displacement is the sum of each individual displacement: ΔdT = Δd1 + Δd2 + …
