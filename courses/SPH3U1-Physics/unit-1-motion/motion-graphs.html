1.5 Motion Graphs

Connecting position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs.

The same motion can be shown three ways. Once you know how to read one, you can sketch the other two.

Motiond-t graphv-t graphVelocityAcceleration
Stopped / stationaryHorizontal lineLine on the v = 0 axisv = 0a = 0
Constant velocityStraight sloped lineHorizontal line (above or below 0)v = constanta = 0
Speeding upCurve getting steeperSloped line moving away from 0|v| increasinga same sign as v
Slowing downCurve flattening outSloped line moving toward 0|v| decreasinga opposite sign to v

Going from d-t to v-t: slope of the d-t graph becomes the height of the v-t graph. Going from v-t to d-t: area under the v-t graph becomes the change in height on the d-t graph.

The Decision Flowchart

When you're handed a v-t graph and asked to describe the motion, work through this:

  1. Is the line horizontal at v = 0? → the object is at rest.
  2. Is the line horizontal but not at v = 0? → constant velocity.
  3. Is the line sloped and moving away from zero (getting more positive or more negative)? → speeding up.
  4. Is the line sloped and moving toward zero? → slowing down.
