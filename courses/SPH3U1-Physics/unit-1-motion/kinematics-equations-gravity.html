1.6 Kinematics Equations & Gravity

The "Big Five" equations, and free fall near Earth's surface.

Each of the Big Five relates four of these five quantities: initial velocity (v1), final velocity (v2), acceleration (a), displacement (Δd), and time (Δt). Each equation leaves exactly one quantity out — that's the clue for which one to use.

  • v2 = v1 + aΔt (no Δd)
  • Δd = v1Δt + ½aΔt² (no v2)
  • Δd = v2Δt − ½aΔt² (no v1)
  • Δd = ½(v1 + v2)Δt (no a)
  • v2² = v1² + 2aΔd (no Δt)

Sign convention: we usually assign + to forward, North, East, up, and right, and − to the opposite directions.

Assume free fall (a = −9.8 m/s², taking down as negative)

Acceleration Due to Gravity

Near Earth's surface, every falling (or thrown) object accelerates downward at g = 9.8 m/s² — as long as we ignore air resistance, which we do for this course. If down is negative in your sign convention, substitute a = −9.8 m/s² into the Big Five.
