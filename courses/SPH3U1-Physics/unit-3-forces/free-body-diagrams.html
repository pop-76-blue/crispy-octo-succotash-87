3.1 Free-Body Diagrams

Drawing every force acting on an object — and finding the net force.

A free-body diagram shows every force acting on one object, drawn as arrows from a single point representing that object. The length of each arrow shows the relative magnitude, and the direction shows which way the force pulls or pushes.

Common Forces

ForceSymbolDirection
Gravity (weight)FgStraight down, toward Earth's centre. Fg = mg
Normal forceFNPerpendicular (90°), away from the surface the object rests on
Applied forceFaWhichever way something is pushing or pulling the object
TensionFTAlong a rope or string, pulling away from the object
FrictionFfAlong the surface, opposing the direction of motion (or attempted motion)
Air resistanceFairOpposing the direction of motion through the air

Once every force is drawn, you find the net force (Fnet) by adding all the forces as vectors — same method as Unit 2 (tip-to-tail, or components). If Fnet = 0, the object is in equilibrium: at rest, or moving at constant velocity. If Fnet ≠ 0, the object accelerates in the direction of the net force.
