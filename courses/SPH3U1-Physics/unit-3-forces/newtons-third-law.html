3.4 Newton's Third Law

For every action, an equal and opposite reaction — but on a different object.

Newton's Third Law: whenever object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts an equal and opposite force back on object A.

The most common mix-up: action-reaction pairs never act on the same object, so they never cancel each other out. (What can cancel on a single object are two different, unrelated balanced forces — like gravity and the normal force on a box sitting on a table. Those are not a Newton's Third Law pair, even though they're also equal and opposite.)
