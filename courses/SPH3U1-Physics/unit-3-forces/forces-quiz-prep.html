Quick Reference

3.1 Types of Forces & FBDs

Every force on one object, drawn as arrows from a single point: gravity, normal, applied, tension, friction, air resistance. Net force = vector sum of all of them.

→ Full review

3.2 Newton's First Law

Objects resist changes in motion (inertia). Fnet = 0 means constant velocity (including rest); otherwise the object accelerates.

→ Full review

3.3 Newton's Second Law

Fnet = ma. Units: N = kg·m/s². Rearrange for a or m as needed.

→ Full review

3.4 Newton's Third Law

Every action force has an equal, opposite reaction force — on a different object. They never cancel each other.

→ Full review

3.5 Using Newton's Laws

Tension is uniform throughout a string; pulleys only redirect it. Connected objects share one acceleration — find it using the total mass, then isolate one object to find a specific tension.

→ Full review

Mixed Practice

Try each on paper first, then reveal the answer.

Free-Body Diagrams
A 5 kg box sits still on a flat table. What two forces act on it, and how do their magnitudes compare?
Gravity (Fg = mg = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N, down) and the normal force (FN, up). Since the box isn't accelerating vertically, FN = Fg = 49 N.
Newton's First Law
A puck slides across frictionless ice at constant velocity. Is there a net force acting on it?
No. Constant velocity (even though it's moving) means Fnet = 0 — that's exactly what Newton's First Law predicts in the absence of friction or other horizontal forces.
Newton's Second Law
A net force of 24 N acts on a 6 kg object. Find its acceleration.
a = Fnet/m = 24/6 = 4 m/s²
Newton's Third Law
A person stands on the ground. Is the pair (gravity pulling the person down) and (the ground pushing the person up) a Newton's Third Law pair?
No. Both of those forces act on the same object (the person), so they're just two balanced forces, not an action-reaction pair. The actual Third Law partner of "the ground pushes up on the person" is "the person pushes down on the ground" — two different forces on two different objects.
Using Newton's Laws
Two objects are tied together with a string resting on a frictionless surface: a 12.0 kg object in front and an 8.0 kg object behind it. A horizontal force of 60.0 N pulls the front object forward. Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the connecting string.
Treat both as one 20.0 kg object: a = Fnet/mtotal = 60.0/20.0 = 3.0 m/s².
Isolate the back object (8.0 kg) — the string is the only horizontal force on it: FT = mback × a = 8.0 × 3.0 = 24 N.
Check it on the Using Newton's Laws tool.
