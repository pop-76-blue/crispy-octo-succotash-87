2.3 Projectile Motion

Horizontal and vertical motion happen independently — at the same time.

Once a projectile leaves its launcher (ignoring air resistance), two things happen at the same time, but independently:

  • Horizontally: no horizontal force acts, so horizontal velocity (vx) stays constant the whole flight.
  • Vertically: gravity accelerates the object downward at g = 9.8 m/s², so vertical velocity (vy) keeps changing — just like the free-fall problems from Unit 1.

The link between the two directions is time — both motions happen over the exact same Δt, so you solve the vertical motion first to find the time of flight, then use that time in the horizontal motion.

Key Formulas

Launched at an angle from the ground (lands at the same height it launched from):

vx = v cosθ    vy0 = v sinθ
time of flight: Δt = 2vy0 / g
max height: hmax = vy0² / (2g)
range: R = vx × Δt

Launched horizontally from a height (e.g. rolling off a table or cliff):

vx = v    vy0 = 0
time of flight: Δt = √(2h / g)
range: R = vx × Δt

Note: combining the final horizontal and vertical velocities into a single "impact velocity" vector is a step beyond these formulas — check with your teacher about whether that's in scope for your test.
