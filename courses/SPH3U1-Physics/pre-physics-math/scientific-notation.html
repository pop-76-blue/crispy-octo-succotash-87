Scientific Notation

A compact way to write very large or very small numbers.

A number in scientific notation always looks like:

a × 10n

where a is a single non-zero digit followed by any decimal places you need (1 ≤ a < 10), and n is an integer (positive for large numbers, negative for small numbers).

e.g. 3 856 = 3.856 × 103, and 0.000 031 = 3.1 × 10-5

Multiplying & Dividing

Multiply: multiply the coefficients, add the exponents.

(2 × 10-4)(8.1 × 10-1) = (2 × 8.1) × 10(-4)+(-1) = 16.2 × 10-5 = 1.62 × 10-4

Divide: divide the coefficients, subtract the exponents.

(7.1 × 106) ÷ (8.2 × 101) = (7.1 ÷ 8.2) × 106-1 ≈ 0.866 × 105 = 8.66 × 104

Powers: raise the coefficient to the power, and multiply the exponent by the power.

(6 × 10-5)3 = 63 × 10-5×3 = 216 × 10-15 = 2.16 × 10-13
