Simplifying Radicals

MCR3U1 · Unit 1 · Section 1.4

A radical is in simplest form when the number under the root has no perfect-square factor left (other than 1).

The procedure

  1. Find the largest perfect square that divides the number (4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, ...).
  2. Split: √(k² × m) = √(k²) × √m.
  3. Take the root of the perfect square: k√m.
  4. If there is a coefficient out front, multiply it by the number you pulled out.
