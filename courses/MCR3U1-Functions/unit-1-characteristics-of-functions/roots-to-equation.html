Finding a Quadratic Equation from Its Roots

MCR3U1 · Unit 1 · Section 1.6

The x-intercepts (roots) tell you the shape of the family; one extra point tells you which parabola in the family. Every parabola with roots r and s is f(x) = a(x − r)(x − s) for some a.

The procedure

  1. Write the factored form with the roots plugged in: f(x) = a(x − r)(x − s).
  2. Substitute the given point (x, y) for x and f(x) and solve for a.
  3. Write the equation in factored form with the value of a.
  4. Expand to get standard form: f(x) = ax² + bx + c.

Irrational roots like p ± √q: the two factors multiply to a square minus a square: (x − p − √q)(x − p + √q) = (x − p)² − q. Expand that and the radicals vanish.
