Simplifying Rational Expressions

MCR3U1 · Unit 2 · Section 2.1

A rational expression is a quotient of two polynomials, like y − 8y − 4. Simplifying means dividing out common factors. It is not defined when the denominator is 0.

The method

  1. Factor the numerator and the denominator completely (common factor first, then difference of squares, trinomial, or grouping).
  2. Divide out factors that appear in both. Only whole factors can cancel, never single terms.
  3. State the restrictions from the original denominator: set each factor equal to 0 and exclude those values.
  4. Check with a convenient value of the variable: the original and the simplified expression should agree.

Restriction finder
