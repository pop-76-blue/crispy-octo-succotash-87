1. Simplifying rational expressions (2.1)

  1. Factor the numerator and denominator completely.
  2. Divide out common factors (never terms).
  3. Restrictions: set every factor of the original denominator to 0.
  4. Check with a convenient value.
x² − 5x + 43x − 12 = (x − 1)(x − 4)3(x − 4) = x − 13,  x ≠ 4

2. Multiplying and dividing (2.2)

  1. Factor everything first.
  2. Division: flip the second fraction.
  3. Divide out factors across the two fractions, then multiply.
  4. Restrictions: every original denominator, and the divisor's numerator.
x² − 25x² − 3x ÷ x + 5x² − 9 = (x − 5)(x + 3)x,  x ≠ 0, 3, −3, −5
Go deeper: Multiply and divide page →

3. Adding and subtracting

  1. Factor the denominators and build the LCD (each factor once).
  2. Rewrite each fraction over the LCD.
  3. Combine numerators. Bracket and distribute the minus sign when subtracting.
  4. Factor, reduce, state restrictions.
2x + 1 + 3x − 2 = 5x − 1(x + 1)(x − 2),  x ≠ −1, 2
Go deeper: Add and subtract page →

4. Where marks get lost

  1. Cancelling terms instead of factors. In x + 5x the x's do not cancel.
  2. Forgetting restrictions, or taking them from the simplified answer. Always use the original denominators, including factors that cancelled.
  3. In division, forgetting that the divisor's numerator also gives a restriction (it ends up in a denominator after you flip).
  4. Not factoring completely, for example leaving a common factor or a difference of squares behind.
  5. Subtracting fractions: forgetting brackets around the second numerator, so the minus sign only hits its first term.
  6. When everything cancels from the top, writing 0 instead of 1.
x + 5x² − 25 = 1x − 5 (not 0),  x ≠ 5, −5
