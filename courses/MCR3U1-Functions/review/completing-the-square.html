Completing the Square

MCR3U1 · Prerequisite Review

Completing the square rewrites y = x² + bx + c as y = (x − h)² + k, which immediately gives you the vertex (h, k) — without graphing anything.

The procedure (leading coefficient = 1)

  1. Take half of b, and square it: (b/2)²
  2. Add and subtract that value inside the expression — this doesn't change its value: x² + bx + (b/2)² − (b/2)² + c
  3. The first three terms are now a perfect square trinomial. Rewrite them as a squared binomial: (x + b/2)²
  4. Combine the leftover constants: y = (x + b/2)² + [c − (b/2)²]
  5. Read off the vertex: h = −b/2, k = c − (b/2)²

Worked example: y = x² + 6x − 1

If the leading coefficient isn't 1

Factor it out of the x² and x terms first, complete the square inside the brackets, then distribute the leading coefficient back in.

Example: y = 2x² + 12x + 5
1. Factor 2 out of the first two terms: y = 2(x² + 6x) + 5
2. Complete the square inside the brackets: half of 6 is 3, 3² = 9 → y = 2(x² + 6x + 9 − 9) + 5
3. Rewrite as a square, keep the −9 outside the brackets for now: y = 2[(x + 3)² − 9] + 5
4. Distribute the 2 back onto both terms inside the brackets: y = 2(x + 3)² − 18 + 5
5. Combine constants: y = 2(x + 3)² − 13 → vertex (−3, −13)
