Circuit Basics — Lab 1 & Lab 2

Unit 3  ·  Concepts behind the simple electric circuit labs

What a simple circuit needs

Every circuit in Lab 1 needs the same three things: a voltage source (a battery), a load that uses the electricity (a lightbulb), and a closed path of wire connecting them. Add a switch in that path and you can open or close it on demand.

Conventional current flow is the convention used throughout this unit: current is treated as flowing from the battery's positive terminal, through the circuit, to the negative terminal. A circuit only lights up when this path is unbroken — that's what "closing the circuit" means.

Batteries in series

Connecting batteries positive-to-negative in a chain (in series) adds their voltages together. Two 1.5V batteries in series supply 3V to the rest of the circuit — which is exactly why Lab 1 has you add a second battery partway through.

Series vs. parallel — the difference that matters

Lab 1 wires each bulb with its own switch, each pair forming an independent branch (parallel). Lab 2 asks you to rebuild the same idea with everything on one single path (series) instead. The difference matters a lot once current is flowing:

Series (Lab 2)Parallel (Lab 1)
Paths for currentExactly one, shared by everythingOne separate branch per bulb
If one switch turns offEvery bulb goes out — the single path is brokenOnly that bulb's branch goes out; the rest stay lit
Current through each bulbThe same everywhere in the loopCan differ, and splits across branches

You'll measure exactly these effects with a multimeter in the labs that come after this one.
