Lab 7n — Solving a Mixed Series-Parallel Circuit

Unit 3  ·  Every tool from Labs 3–7, combined into one circuit

Lab 7n gives you a circuit and asks you to find every unknown voltage, current, and the total resistance. There's no single formula for that — it's a combination of everything so far: the series/parallel resistance formulas (Lab 6), Ohm's Law (Lab 7), and Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (Lab 7a). This page walks through the full solution, step by step, then lets you change the numbers and try your own version.

The circuit

R1 is in series with the parallel combination of R2 and R3. Battery supplies 12.0V.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Combine the parallel section first
    R2 and R3 are in parallel with each other, so replace them with one equivalent resistance before doing anything else.
    Rparallel = (R2 × R3) / (R2 + R3) = (6 × 13) / 19 ≈ 4.105Ω
  2. Find the total resistance
    Now the circuit is just R1 in series with that one equivalent resistor.
    RT = R1 + Rparallel = 1 + 4.105 ≈ 5.105Ω
  3. Find the total current (I1)
    With RT known, Ohm's Law gives the current the battery pushes out — which is also I1, since R1 carries all of it before the split.
    I1 = V / RT = 12 / 5.105 ≈ 2.351A
  4. Find the voltage drop across R1 (V1)
    Ohm's Law again, now applied just to R1.
    V1 = I1 × R1 = 2.351 × 1 ≈ 2.351V
  5. Find the voltage across the parallel section (V2 = V3)
    Kirchhoff's Voltage Law: the supply voltage is the sum of every drop in the loop. Whatever V1 didn't use, the parallel section gets — and since R2 and R3 are in parallel with each other, they share that exact same voltage.
    V2 = V3 = V − V1 = 12 − 2.351 ≈ 9.649V
  6. Find I2 and I3
    Ohm's Law one more time, separately for each parallel branch, since they share voltage but not current.
    I2 = V2 / R2 = 9.649 / 6 ≈ 1.608A  ·  I3 = V3 / R3 = 9.649 / 13 ≈ 0.742A
  7. Check your work
    The two branch currents should add back up to the total current that went through R1 — this is current conservation, the same rule from Lab 4.
    I2 + I3 = 1.608 + 0.742 ≈ 2.351A = I1 ✓
