Lab 9a — what's inside a 7408

A 7408 is a "quad AND" chip: one 14-leg package holding four separate AND gates. (The slides show both 74LS08 and 74HC08 — two versions of the same chip, with the same legs and the same AND logic.) In Lab 5 you built AND from two switches in series. A chip does the same job electrically: voltage on the input legs decides whether current comes out of the output leg, following the AND truth table.

Lab 9b — P AND Q on a real chip

Two dip switches stand for P and Q (ON = 1/True, OFF = 0/False). They feed legs 1 and 2; the gate's output, leg 3, drives the LED through a 200Ω resistor. Each switch also has a 1 kΩ "drainage" resistor to ground: when the switch is OFF, that resistor pulls the chip input down to a definite 0 V instead of leaving it unconnected, because an unconnected (floating) input can read as 0 or 1 unpredictably. When a switch is ON, a small current (about 6 V ÷ 1 kΩ = 6 mA) also drains through its resistor — harmless, and the reason the resistors are as big as 1 kΩ.

Step 7 says to measure the LED current and check it is between 12 mA and 20 mA. With the numbers worked out below you should see about 16 mA.

Lab 9c — where did the other 0.75 V go?

The LED branch has three things in series: the chip's 45Ω, the 200Ω resistor, and the LED. The same current flows through all three, so KVL says their drops add up to the 6 V battery.

Question 2: at 16 mA, the resistor drops 0.016 × 200 = 3.2 V. The LED itself drops about 2.05 V, so the LED + resistor combination drops 3.2 + 2.05 ≈ 5.25 V.

Question 3: 6 − 5.25 = 0.75 V is left over. It is dropped across the chip's internal 45Ω: V = I × R = 0.016 × 45 = 0.72 V ≈ 0.75 V. Same current, extra resistance in series, extra voltage drop.

Lab 10a — P AND Q AND R

One gate only has two inputs, so a three-variable AND chains two gates: (P AND Q) AND R. The first gate (legs 1, 2 → leg 3) combines P and Q; its output then feeds one input of the second gate (legs 4, 5 → leg 6), with R on the other input; leg 6 drives the LED. Chaining works because AND gives the same answer whichever pair you combine first. Check your wiring against the slide picture: leg 3 must connect on to the second gate's input.

The LED resistor is yours to work out (or read from its colour bands). Using the Lab 9c idea: R = (Vbattery − VLED) / I − 45Ω. For about 16 mA on 6 V that is (6 − 2.05) / 0.016 − 45 ≈ 202Ω, so the same 200Ω as Lab 9b works; the slide's meter shows 15.9 mA. The Circuit Calculators LED tab now has a field for the chip's resistance.
