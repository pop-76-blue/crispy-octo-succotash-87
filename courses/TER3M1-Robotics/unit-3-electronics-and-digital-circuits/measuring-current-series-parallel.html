Measuring Current — Series vs. Parallel

Unit 3  ·  Lab 3 (Definitions & Formulas) · Labs 3a–e & 4a–k (Measuring Current)

You already know the rule from Lab 3: to measure current, the multimeter must always be placed in series with the component, never in parallel. Labs 3a–e and 4a–k use that rule to reveal two very different behaviors, depending on how the rest of the circuit is wired.

Series circuits: current is identical everywhere

In a series loop there's only one path, so the same current passes every point — before the first bulb, between any two bulbs, or coming back into the battery. Set one value and watch it appear everywhere.

Parallel circuits: current splits, then sums back up

Each parallel branch gets its own share of current. Whatever leaves the battery is exactly the sum of every branch, and that same total is what returns.

Mixed circuits (Lab 4e)

Lab 4e wires bulbs 1 and 2 in series with each other, then puts that whole pair in parallel with bulb 3. That's not a contradiction of the two rules above — it's both at once: bulbs 1 and 2 share one current (they're in series with each other), and that shared value becomes just one branch's current in the parallel sum with bulb 3. The calculator above still applies — you'd just enter the series pair's shared current as a single branch.

Try this: In the parallel calculator, enter 60 mA for two branches and predict the total before checking (it should match Lab 4c's 120 mA). Then add a third branch of 60 mA and predict the new total — this is exactly the setup in Lab 4d.
