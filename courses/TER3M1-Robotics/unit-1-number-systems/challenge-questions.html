1.4 Exercise 4

Why does 30016 = 1002 × 3008?

At first this looks like a coincidence — three completely different bases producing an equation that balances. It isn't a coincidence: it falls out of the fact that 16 = 24, so 16² = 28. Splitting that exponent as 2² × 26 gives you exactly a base-2 term (1002 = 2²) and a base-8 term (26 = 8², so 3008 = 3 × 8² carries the "3" and the 8² part). The leading digit just rides along for the whole calculation.

1.4 Exercise 11, part a

Why Hex Bytes Concatenate the Way They Do

For any hex digits m, n, o, p, q, r, s, t: mn:op:qr:st = shift-left-2-bytes(mn:op) + qr:st. In other words, gluing a 4-byte hex number together is the same as taking its upper 2 bytes, shifting them left by 2 bytes (which is the same as multiplying by 164, since 2 bytes = 4 hex digits), and adding the lower 2 bytes back in. This is exactly the SHL operation you already know, just working in units of whole bytes instead of single bits.
