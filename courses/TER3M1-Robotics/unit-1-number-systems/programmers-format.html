Programmer's Format & Bit Grouping

Unit 1  ·  1.2.4 Using a Programmer's Calculator to Convert Bases

Raw binary conversion gives you the fewest bits possible — but real systems (RAM addresses, network packets, anything a "programmer's calculator" displays) pad every number out to a full byte, or several bytes, and add a space every 4 bits so it's readable. This page shows both forms side by side for any number you enter.

Worked example

Take decimal 27. Converted to binary it's 11011 — only 5 bits. Since real systems work in whole bytes, we pad with leading zeroes until the length is a multiple of 8: 00011011. Then, purely for human readability, a space is inserted every 4 bits: 0001 1011. The value hasn't changed — only how it's displayed.

Try checking your answers against a real programmer's calculator such as the one at calc.penjee.com, or your operating system's built-in calculator switched to "Programmer" mode.
