The counting rules

1. Nonzero digits

Every nonzero digit always counts.

248 → 3 sig figs

2. Captive zeros

Zeros between two nonzero digits always count.

3004 → 4 sig figs

3. Leading zeros

Zeros before the first nonzero digit are never significant — they just place the decimal.

0.0857 → 3 sig figs

4. Trailing zeros — with a decimal point

If the number has a decimal point, trailing zeros are significant.

0.0900 → 3 sig figs

5. Trailing zeros — no decimal point

Without a decimal point, trailing zeros are not counted as significant.

210 → 2 sig figs

6. Scientific notation

Every digit shown in the coefficient is significant — that's the whole point of writing it that way.

4.10 × 10⁻¹ → 3 sig figs

Rounding calculated answers

Adding / Subtracting

Round the answer to the fewest decimal places of any number in the problem.

27.66 + 3.1 → answer has 1 decimal place

Multiplying / Dividing

Round the answer to the fewest sig figs of any number in the problem.

27.66 × 3.1 → answer has 2 sig figs
