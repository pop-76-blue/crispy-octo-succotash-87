Naming Ionic Compounds

Unit 2: Chemical Nomenclature · Fixed-charge binaries and the Stock system for multivalent metals.

This unit uses the Stock system (Roman numerals) for multivalent metals — not the classical "-ous/-ic" naming system, and no organic naming either.

Binary ionic compounds (fixed charge)

Naming: metal's full name + nonmetal's stem + "-ide". Example: Al₂O₃ → aluminum oxide.
Writing the formula (cross-over rule): write each ion's charge as a subscript on the other ion, then reduce to the lowest whole-number ratio. Example: Mg²⁺ and N³⁻ → Mg₃N₂ (charges cross over as subscripts).

Multivalent metals (Stock system)

Some metals can form more than one stable ion. The Roman numeral after the metal's name tells you which charge is being used in that compound — it does not change the metal's name.

To find the charge when you're given a formula, work backward: the total positive charge must balance the total negative charge. Example: in Fe₂S₃, sulfide is always 2−, so 3 S = 6− total, meaning the 2 Fe must together supply 6+, so each Fe is 3+ → iron(III) sulfide.
