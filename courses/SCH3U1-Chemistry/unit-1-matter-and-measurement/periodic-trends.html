Periodic Trends

Unit 1, Lesson 4 · How atomic properties change as you move across and down the periodic table.

Periodic table organization

Rows (periods) = number of occupied energy shells.
Columns (groups/families) = number of valence electrons (for main-group elements).
Group 1 — Alkali metals (very reactive)
Group 2 — Alkaline earth metals (reactive)
Groups 3–12 — Transition metals
Group 17 — Halogens (very reactive)
Group 18 — Noble gases (stable/unreactive)
Lanthanides / Actinides — the two rows set below the main table

What drives the trends

Two competing effects explain every trend below:
Nuclear charge — the pull of the nucleus on the valence shell (increases as you add protons).
Shielding — inner-shell electrons blocking that pull (increases as you add energy shells).

The four properties

PropertyDefinitionAcross a period →Down a group ↓
Atomic radiusSize of the atomDecreases (more nuclear pull, same shielding)Increases (new shell added each row)
Ionization energyEnergy to remove an electronIncreases (held more tightly)Decreases (outer e⁻ is farther away, more shielded)
ElectronegativityPull on shared (bonded) electronsIncreases (except noble gases)Decreases
Electron affinityEnergy released adding an electronIncreases (more negative/more energy released)Decreases
Ions vs. neutral atoms: cations (+ ions) are smaller than their neutral atom — losing an electron often means losing a whole outer shell. Anions (− ions) are larger than their neutral atom — the same nuclear charge now has to hold onto more electrons, so it pulls each one in a little less tightly.
