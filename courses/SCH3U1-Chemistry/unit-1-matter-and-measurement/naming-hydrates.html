Naming Hydrates

Unit 2: Chemical Nomenclature · Salts with water built into their crystal structure.

A hydrate is an ionic compound with a specific number of water molecules trapped in its crystal structure. The water is loosely held and can be driven off with gentle heating, leaving the anhydrous (water-free) salt behind.

Naming pattern

Name the ionic compound normally, then add a numerical prefix (same mono-di-tri list as molecular compounds) + "hydrate" for the number of water molecules.
Example: MgSO₄ · 7H₂O → magnesium sulfate heptahydrate.
FormulaName
CuSO₄ · 5H₂OCopper(II) sulfate pentahydrate
CoCl₂ · 6H₂OCobalt(II) chloride hexahydrate
Na₂CO₃ · 10H₂OSodium carbonate decahydrate
