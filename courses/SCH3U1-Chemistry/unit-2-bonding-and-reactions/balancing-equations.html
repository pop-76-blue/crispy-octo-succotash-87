Balancing Chemical Equations

Unit 5: Chemical Reactions · Conservation of mass — same atoms in, same atoms out.

The law of conservation of mass: atoms aren't created or destroyed in a chemical reaction, just rearranged. A balanced equation has the same number of each type of atom on both sides.

Tips

  • Only change the big numbers in front of formulas (coefficients) — never the little subscripts inside a formula. Changing a subscript makes a different substance entirely.
  • Balance one element at a time; save lone elements (like O₂ or a single metal) for last.
  • Treat an unchanged polyatomic ion (like SO₄²⁻) as one unit if it appears on both sides.
  • If you end up with fractions, multiply every coefficient by the same number to clear them.
  • Count the total number of each atom on both sides as a final check.
