VSEPR Theory

Unit 4: Bonding & Molecular Shape · Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion.

Big idea: electron pairs (bonding or lone) around a central atom repel each other and spread out as far apart as possible — that spacing gives the molecule its shape. Lone pairs take up more space than bonding pairs, which compresses the bond angles below the "ideal" value.
A note on scope: some of this material (5, 6, and 7 electron domains) goes beyond what a 4-domain-capped quiz would cover — check with your own notes/teacher for what's testable right now.

General method

  1. Draw the Lewis structure and count bonding pairs (bp) and lone pairs (lp) around the central atom. A single, double, or triple bond all count as one bonding pair/domain.
  2. Total electron pairs (ep) = bp + lp. Use that total to find the base electron geometry.
  3. Lone pairs modify the shape's name and compress the bond angle, but don't change the electron geometry.
