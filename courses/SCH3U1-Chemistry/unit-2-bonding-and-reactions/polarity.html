Bond polarity

Electronegativity (EN) measures how strongly an atom pulls on shared (bonded) electrons. The bigger the EN difference between two bonded atoms, the more unevenly the electrons are shared.

Molecular polarity

A bond being polar doesn't automatically make the whole molecule polar — shape matters. A molecule is polar only if it has an overall (net) dipole: one end more positive, the other more negative.

Quick checklist — a molecule is usually nonpolar if: the central atom has no lone pairs, AND all the outer atoms are the same (symmetric arrangement) — the individual bond dipoles point in different directions and cancel out.
It's usually polar if there's a lone pair on the central atom, or the outer atoms are different — the bond dipoles don't fully cancel, leaving a net dipole.

Compare: CO₂ vs. H₂O

Linear, 180°. The two C=O bond dipoles point in exactly opposite directions and are equal in size — they cancel completely. Nonpolar molecule, even though each individual bond is polar.

Bent, ~104.5°. The two O–H bond dipoles don't point opposite each other (and there are lone pairs on the central O), so they add up to a net dipole. Polar molecule.
