Types of Chemical Reactions

Unit 5: Chemical Reactions · The six families, plus the sub-patterns that predict products.

The six types

Synthesis

Two or more substances combine into one.
A + B → AB

Decomposition

One compound breaks into two or more simpler substances.
AB → A + B

Single displacement

One element swaps places with part of a compound.
A + BC → AC + B

Double displacement

Two ionic compounds swap partners.
AB + CD → AD + CB

Combustion

A substance burns in oxygen, usually producing CO₂ and H₂O (for hydrocarbons).
CₓHᵧ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Acid-base (neutralization)

An acid and a base react to form water + a salt.
HA + BOH → H₂O + BA

Useful sub-patterns

  • Non-metal oxide + water → acid (e.g. CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃)
  • Metal oxide + water → base (e.g. Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH)
  • Double displacement → decomposition: when the "new" product from a double displacement is unstable, it immediately decomposes further.
    • acid + carbonate → carbonic acid + salt → decomposes to CO₂ + H₂O + salt
    • acid + sulphite → sulphurous acid + salt → decomposes to SO₂ + H₂O + salt
    • ammonium salt + hydroxide → ammonium hydroxide + salt → decomposes to NH₃ + H₂O + salt
